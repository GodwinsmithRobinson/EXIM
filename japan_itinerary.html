1. Trip Overview & Essential Pre-Trip Information

Why Japan for a First International Trip?

Japan is one of the safest, cleanest and most family-friendly destinations in the world — an ideal first international trip. Trains run on the minute, hospitals are world-class, baby facilities exist in almost every public space, and the Japanese people are exceptionally kind and helpful to families with young children.

Key Japan Facts for First-Time Visitors

  • Safety: Japan is consistently ranked one of the world’s safest countries. Crime rate is extremely low.
  • Cleanliness: Streets, toilets, trains and restaurants are impeccably clean — perfect for a 7-month baby.
  • Baby-Friendliness: Multi-functional nursing rooms (授乳室, jyunyuushitsu) are available in shopping malls, train stations, department stores and major attractions — always with diaper-changing tables, nursing chairs and a sink.
  • Cash Culture: Japan is still largely a cash society. Carry ¥10,000–¥20,000 in cash daily. You can withdraw from 7-Eleven and Japan Post ATMs using international Visa/Mastercard.
  • Tap Water: 100% safe to drink and use for mixing baby formula.
  • Electricity: 100V, Type A plugs (same as USA). Carry a universal adapter.
  • SIM / Internet: Buy a tourist data SIM at the airport (IIJmio, Rakuten, or B-Mobile) or rent a pocket Wi-Fi at arrival. Approximately ¥3,000–¥5,000 (~₹1,650–₹2,750) for 5 days.

Important Visa Notes

  • Apply for all family members separately — each person needs their own visa application.
  • The infant (7 months) also requires a separate visa. Carry the birth certificate.
  • Travel Insurance is mandatory. Recommended coverage: ₹50 lakh (~USD 60,000) per person.
  • Once visa is stamped, you will receive a sticker visa in your passport valid for 3 months from the date of issue.

Budget Tips: - Book 3–4 months in advance for best fares. - Travel in shoulder season (September–November, or late January–February) for lowest airfares. - Infant (under 2 years): Travels free on lap (no seat) on most airlines on international flights. 7. Day 3 — Tokyo Disneyland (Full Day)

The Highlight of Your Trip!

Tokyo Disneyland is widely considered the world’s best Disneyland — it is cleaner, more organised and more magical than any other Disney park globally. For your 7-year-old, this will be an unforgettable experience.

Important: Tokyo Disneyland and Tokyo DisneySea are operated by Oriental Land Company (OLC), not Disney US. Tickets must be booked online in advance — walk-up purchase is possible but can lead to sellouts on peak days.

Getting Around with a Pram / Stroller

  • All major Tokyo Metro and JR stations have elevators (look for blue elevator signs)
  • Priority seating sections on trains — most Japanese people will happily give up seats
  • Pram-friendly routes: Use the Ginza, Hibiya or Yamanote lines — wider carriages
  • Tokyo Bus system also accommodates prams — use the middle door

Taxis & Uber

  • Taxis in Tokyo are clean, safe and drivers are professional
  • No tipping in Japan — taxis or restaurants
  • Uber works in Tokyo and accepts international credit cards
  • For family with infant + luggage: request a Minivan/Van option on Uber

Budget Optimization Tips

  • Book flights 3–4 months in advance and travel mid-week (Tuesday/Wednesday) for lowest fares.
  • Use Booking.com or Agoda for hotel deals; or book directly through hotel websites for best rates.
  • Use IC cards instead of single-journey tickets to save 5–10% on metro fares.
  • Eat at convenience stores and ramen shops for 1–2 meals daily — excellent quality at ¥500–¥800 per person.
  • Buy Disneyland tickets online (KKday/Klook in INR) — no extra currency conversion loss.
  • Avoid taxi except for baby-critical situations — Tokyo trains are safe, punctual and far cheaper.
  • Buy duty-free at Narita/Haneda for cosmetics, chocolates and Japanese whisky.
  • Look for family discounts: Many Tokyo attractions offer 2+1 family passes or free child entry under 6.
